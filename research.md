# My research notes

## The problem I am exploring

From conversations with classmates, I heard that some relatives forget medicines while busy with household work, commuting, or other daily responsibilities.
I would like to find out whether a simple reminder tool could help someone notice patterns in their routine.
My mother is the person I hope to ask to try the prototype, but I have not recorded a test with her yet.
The prototype summarizes a person's own check-ins by everyday context and is designed not to store medicine names, doses, diagnoses, or prescription images.
I still need to learn whether the people I want to help find this useful, which reminder methods they already use, and what information they are comfortable recording.

## Existing options I considered

1. **Medication reminder apps, such as Medisafe.** The GoodRx article describes apps where people enter medication schedules and receive alerts; some also offer adherence logs, caregiver reports, or refill reminders. This means reminders are not a new idea. I have not yet asked my intended tester whether she already uses an app, finds setup difficult, or is comfortable with its data practices. I need to check whether my prototype addresses a real need rather than assume that it does.

2. **Pill organizers and automatic dispensers.** MedlinePlus describes day/time compartments and automatic dispensers with alarms. I have not tested how well these options fit my intended tester's routine, so I cannot claim my prototype is better. I need to learn whether there is a practical gap before deciding what to build next.

## Sources

### GoodRx — “Medication Reminder Apps: 6 Options to Help You Stay on Track”

- URL: https://www.goodrx.com/healthcare-access/digital-health/medication-reminder-apps
- I noted that the article describes schedule-based alerts and, for some apps, adherence logs, caregiver reports, and refill reminders.
- This helped me see that my project needs a specific, user-confirmed purpose; I should not present medication reminders as a new feature.

### MedlinePlus — “Organizing Your Medicines”

- URL: https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000600.htm
- I noted the guide's examples of pill organizers, automatic dispensers with alarms, color marks, and medication records.
- The guide also says to contact a health provider about difficulty remembering medicines and not to change or stop medicines on one's own. That is why my prototype stays descriptive and gives no dosing advice.

I still need to read both sources myself and check that these notes reflect my understanding before submitting them as my research.
